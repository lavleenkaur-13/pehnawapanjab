The Complete Punjabi Jutti Care Guide

Published 12 May 2026

A well-made Punjabi jutti is meant to last for years — softening with wear, moulding to your foot, and developing a beautiful patina that machine-made shoes simply cannot achieve. Here's how to care for yours so they reach their full lifespan.

Breaking In New Juttis

New handcrafted juttis can feel stiff initially. This is normal — the leather is dense and the soles haven't yet moulded to your feet. Resist the urge to return them. Here's how to break them in correctly:

  1. Start with short wear sessions. Wear them around the house for 30-60 minutes at a time for the first week. Don't take them on a long event yet.
  2. Use a leather conditioner. Apply a small amount of natural leather conditioner (or even a drop of mustard oil, the traditional Punjabi remedy) to the inside of the heel area. This softens the leather faster.
  3. Wear thick socks indoors. Pull on a pair of thick cotton socks and wear your juttis with them for a few hours. This gently stretches the leather without causing blisters.
  4. Be patient. Most juttis fully break in within 2-3 weeks of regular wear. Don't force the process.

Daily Care

Once your juttis are broken in, regular maintenance keeps them in top condition:

  • Wipe them down after each wear. Use a soft, dry cotton cloth to remove dust, especially from around the embroidery where dirt can collect.
  • Use a shoe horn. Always use a shoe horn to put on juttis — this prevents the back of the heel from collapsing inward, which is the most common way juttis get permanently damaged.
  • Rotate pairs. If you have multiple juttis, rotate them. Leather needs 24 hours between wears to dry out and rebound.
  • Watch where you walk. Juttis are designed for dry surfaces. Avoid wet grass, puddles, or oily kitchen floors.

Storage

How you store your juttis matters as much as how you wear them:

  • Always use the dust bag your juttis came in. If you've lost it, any breathable cotton pouch works.
  • Stuff them with paper. Crumpled tissue paper (not newspaper — the ink transfers) inside each jutti helps them hold their shape during storage.
  • Avoid plastic. Never store leather juttis in plastic bags or sealed containers. Leather needs to breathe; trapped moisture causes mold and a permanent musty smell.
  • Keep them out of sunlight. Direct sunlight fades the embroidery threads and dries out the leather. A cool, dark cupboard is ideal.
  • Add a desiccant in humid climates. A small silica gel packet or a piece of charcoal in the storage box absorbs excess moisture.

Cleaning Embroidery

The embroidery on your juttis is the most delicate part. Treat it carefully:

  • Use a soft brush. A clean, soft toothbrush is perfect for gently brushing dust out of embroidery patterns.
  • Never use water directly. Water can cause the silk threads to bleed colour. If you must clean a stain, use a barely-damp cotton swab and dab gently.
  • Treat zari work even more carefully. Metallic zari threads tarnish quickly. Wipe with a dry microfiber cloth only.
  • For mirror work, use a small cotton swab to clean around each mirror. Avoid getting any moisture on the adhesive.

Common Repairs

Even well-cared-for juttis sometimes need small repairs. Here's what's worth fixing and what isn't:

  • Worn-out sole? Take them to a traditional mochi (cobbler) — many specialize in jutti resoling. A good resoling can extend a jutti's life by another 3-5 years.
  • Loose embroidery thread? Don't pull it. Carefully snip it close to the fabric, or take the jutti to your jutti maker for proper repair.
  • Stretched out? Stuff with tissue paper and store for a few weeks. Leather often rebounds. If not, a cobbler can sometimes tighten the heel.
  • Crack across the upper? This usually means the leather has dried out completely. Conditioning may help, but extensive cracking generally means the jutti has reached the end of its life.

A Final Word

The best-cared-for juttis become heirlooms. A well-made pair, treated with respect, can easily last 5-10 years of regular wear. They develop character — slight softening, patina, a unique imprint of the foot they've served. This is the opposite of disposable fashion. This is heritage footwear, made by hands that have practiced the craft for generations.

Treat your juttis like the small works of art they are. They'll repay you with years of comfortable, beautiful wear.
